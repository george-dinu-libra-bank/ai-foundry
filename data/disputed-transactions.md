# Disputed transactions and chargebacks

A dispute is a customer's formal claim that a card transaction should not stand. It is not
the same as a fraud report: a fraud report is about a card being used by someone else, a
dispute may also be about a merchant who never delivered.

## Deadlines

The customer must raise the dispute within **13 months** of the transaction date. Inside
that window there is a second, tighter deadline: a claim about an **unauthorised**
transaction must be raised **without undue delay and no later than 60 days** after the
transaction appeared on the statement, or the bank may reject the provisional refund even
if the underlying claim is valid.

## The procedure

1. **Take the claim.** Record the transaction date, the amount, the merchant name as it
   appears on the statement, and the reason category: unauthorised, not delivered, not as
   described, duplicate charge, wrong amount, or cancelled subscription still charging.

2. **Check the obvious explanations first.** A large share of disputes are the customer not
   recognising a trading name, a delayed capture posting days after the purchase, or a
   family member using the card. Ask before opening a case.

3. **For "not delivered" or "not as described", require contact with the merchant first.**
   The card schemes require evidence that the customer tried to resolve it directly. Record
   the date of that contact and the merchant's answer, or the absence of one after
   15 calendar days.

4. **Open the case** in the disputes system and give the customer the case reference.

5. **Apply the provisional refund** where the claim is for an unauthorised transaction and
   the customer's account of events is plausible. The provisional refund is credited within
   **1 working day** of the case opening and is reversed if the dispute is later lost.

6. **Block or replace the card** if the reason is unauthorised use. A card whose details
   are compromised must be replaced, not merely re-PINned.

7. **Wait for the scheme process.** The merchant's bank has **30 calendar days** to respond.
   Total resolution takes up to **45 calendar days** for domestic transactions and up to
   **90 calendar days** for cross-border ones.

8. **Communicate the outcome in writing.** A rejected claim must state which evidence
   defeated it.

## Fees

Raising a dispute is free. A dispute rejected as **unfounded** — meaning the customer's
own account of events was contradicted by the evidence, not merely that the merchant won —
carries the dispute fee in the current fee schedule. The fee is not charged where the
customer acted in good faith.

## What cannot be disputed

Cash withdrawn at an ATM where the machine dispensed the correct amount, transactions
authorised with a correct PIN and no evidence of coercion, and gambling transactions the
customer regrets. Buyer's remorse is not a dispute reason under any scheme.
